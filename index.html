THE PHYSICS, MADE VISIBLE

Small insertions.
Remarkable evolution.

The characteristic matrix of each lossless layer gives the reflectance at normal incidence. All thickness derivatives and needle sensitivities are calculated analytically.

  1. Refine. Levenberg–Marquardt adjusts the geometric layer thicknesses.
  2. Insert. A negative needle derivative identifies a promising replacement of host material. A thin layer is inserted and refined.
  3. Grow. One layer moves to its nearest thicker local minimum, opening a new search region. Merit may temporarily increase.
  4. Clean up. Very thin layers are removed and adjacent equal materials merge.
  5. Polish. A final thickness optimization returns the best design found.

Merit F = mean[(R − Rtarget)²]. Optical thickness = Σ nᵢdᵢ. Layer order is air → substrate. Limits are enforced; an arbitrary target may be unattainable. Results are local numerical solutions, with no claim of global optimality.

Based on the supplied needle-method notes; verified against Alexander Tikhonravov, Optical Coatings: Design, Characterization, Monitoring (SPIE Press, 2024), and Furman & Tikhonravov, Basics of Optics of Multilayer Systems (1992). See the repository methodology for equations and references.
